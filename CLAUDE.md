# bm

Huge rule for this game: V = Velscour = the bad guy (essentially Satan). T = Tessariel = the good guy (essentially Jesus). They don't go together. Anything with a V is never found anywhere near anything with a T. Velscour's church, the cathedral and its catacombs, carries V items only. See "The two sides" in README.md.

Auto-play (the Auto button under the skull, or P; `src/ui/autobrain.js`) plays the whole game, so it has to be kept up with it. Whenever you add or change quests or their steps, foes, monster groups or lairs, places, spells, shops or items, teach auto-play about them in `src/ui/autobrain.js` and run `node tools/autoplay/check.mjs`, which fails until it knows them all. Then watch it play the new part. See "Auto-play" in README.md.

Gear sets for the third-person wizard come back from the owner as zips made by the skin kit (`set.json`, `color.jpg`, `preview.jpg` and `source/` inside). To bring them in: save each zip, run `python tools/player/skins/import_set.py <zips>`, look at each set's `preview.jpg`, check nothing on it breaks the V and T rule above (no letters or monograms, none of Velscour's marks on the hero), run `node --test tests/body-skin.test.mjs`, and commit. A set whose `wears` is empty isn't worn by anything yet: ask the owner what it's for. A set with a rarity (in its `set.json`, or the end of its id) becomes five items of its own with set bonuses; the importer writes `src/items/robesets.js` for that, so run `node tools/items/check.mjs` too. See "Gear on the body" in README.md.

What a monster throws or marks on the ground goes through `sim.perils` (`src/sim/perils.js`), by a named move in `src/sim/moves.js` where there is one: don't write a one-off hazard. A new kind of Tripo monster gets its tricks in `TRICKS` there (`node --test tests/perils.test.mjs` fails until it has). After changing a fight in the Warrens, play it out with `node tools/dungeon/fight.mjs` (add `--slow`, and `--still`, which should die). See "Monsters' attacks" in README.md.

Painted art for the interface comes back from the owner as a zip made with the UI kit (`art/ui/bellmourn_ui_kit.zip`; what `scripts/check.py` in it wrote, `bellmourn_ui_art.zip`, or a folder of sheets named for their ids). To bring it in: save the zip, run `python tools/ui/import_kit.py <zip>`, look at every contact sheet it writes to `tools/build/ui_import/`, check nothing on them breaks the V and T rule above (no letters or monograms at all, neither side's marks on the frames and buttons everything shares), look at the game wearing it on a phone's screen and a desk's, run `node --test tests/touch-hud.test.mjs`, and commit `assets/ui/kit/` and `art/ui/sheets/`. Any part of the kit may come back; the game draws the rest itself. Where anything sits on a phone is one table, `src/ui/thumbs.js`: change that, not the stylesheet's numbers, and the same test says if anything overlaps. A new button or menu icon is drawn in `src/ui/icons.js` and added to a sheet in `tools/ui/pieces.mjs` (then `node tools/ui/build_kit.mjs`, which remakes the kit). See "The interface, and a phone's screen" and "The art kit" in README.md.

A phone's browser kills a page that holds too much memory: the screen goes white and the game starts again, with nothing said. After adding anything that's loaded at the start or kept while the game runs (models, pictures, sound, scenery, anything built into big lists), run `NODE_PATH=$(npm root -g) node tools/phone_memory.mjs` with the server up and compare it with the table under "When the browser closes the game" in README.md; `TOP=20` and `WHO=1` say which code holds what. Geometry is built in typed arrays and let go once it's on the GPU, the simulation builds none (`buildSites()`), a delve's corners are on the GPU only while you're down it (`buildUnder`), and sound that's stopped being heard is dropped (`src/audio.js`).
